GST Compliance, common pitfalls

RDR Team
| 5 min read

The Goods and Services Tax (GST) has revolutionized the Indian taxation system, bringing uniformity and transparency. However, for many businesses, navigating the complexities of GST compliance remains a challenge. Staying compliant is not just about avoiding penalties; it's about ensuring smooth business operations and maintaining a good reputation.

Why Compliance Matters

Non-compliance can lead to severe consequences, including hefty fines, interest on delayed payments, and even the suspension of your GST registration. Moreover, a low compliance rating can affect your relationship with vendors and customers who may be wary of dealing with non-compliant entities.

Common Pitfalls

Here are some of the most common mistakes businesses make regarding GST:

1. Incorrect HSN/SAC Codes

Classifying goods and services under the wrong HSN (Harmonized System of Nomenclature) or SAC (Services Accounting Code) is a frequent error. This leads to incorrect tax rates being applied, which can result in demand notices from the tax department.

2. Mismatched Input Tax Credit (ITC)

Claiming ITC that doesn't match the GSTR-2A/2B data is a major red flag. Businesses often claim credit based on their purchase register without verifying if the supplier has uploaded the invoices. This can lead to the reversal of credit along with interest.

3. Ignoring Reverse Charge Mechanism (RCM)

Many businesses overlook their liability under RCM, especially when dealing with unregistered suppliers or specific categories of services like legal fees or goods transport agency services.

4. Filing Returns with Mismatched Data

Discrepancies between GSTR-1 (outward supplies) and GSTR-3B (summary return) are automated triggers for scrutiny. It is crucial to ensure that the data reported in both returns aligns perfectly.

Best Practices

To ensure compliance, conducting regular internal audits is essential. Utilizing robust accounting software that checks for errors can also be a game-changer. Furthermore, staying updated with the latest notifications and circulars from the GST Council is mandatory for every finance team.

Conclusion

GST compliance is an ongoing process that requires diligence and attention to detail. By being aware of these common pitfalls and implementing strong checks and balances, businesses can mitigate risks and focus on growth.
